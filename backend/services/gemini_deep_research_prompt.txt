  A new Looper's "problem" is often a misunderstanding of Loop's insulin model, whereas an existing Looper's problem is one of tuning. 
  The software must differentiate.Context 1: New Looper (e.g., < 1 month of data)Analysis: When moving from manual pumping to Loop, 
  settings will "most likely need to be adjusted".2 Loop's insulin model includes a long, low-level "tail".2 If a user's manual-pumping 
  ISF is too "strong" (a low number), Loop's algorithm will see the "tail" and stack insulin, leading to over-corrections.Suggestion: 
  "As a new Looper, you may be seeing over-corrections. Many users find they need to raise the number for their ISF (e.g., from 50 to 55)

  Please use your judgment and be prepared to roll back this change."Context 2: Existing Looper (Tuning & the CSF Constraint)Analysis: 
  For an established Looper, ISF and CR are linked. The ratio of ISF to CR is the Carb Sensitivity Factor (CSF), which represents the BG 
  impact of 1 gram of glucose. This CSF "should be consistent".2 Therefore, ISF and CR changes should "move in the same direction".2Suggestion 
  (if CR and ISF need adjustment): "The analysis suggests your settings may need to be stronger (or weaker). The loopnlearn.org community
